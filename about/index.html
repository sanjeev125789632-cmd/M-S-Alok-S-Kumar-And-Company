How an engagement is handled

Compliance work goes wrong in predictable ways: a return filed on incomplete data, an input tax credit claimed without checking the supplier's filing, a deed drafted from a template that does not match what the parties actually agreed. Our approach is designed around avoiding those failures rather than fixing them later.

  1. Understand the facts first. Before we quote, we look at the actual books, registrations and past filings. A ten-minute review often changes the scope entirely.
  2. Say plainly what the law requires. You get a clear answer on what is allowed, what is risky, and what will not survive scrutiny at assessment — in plain language, not section numbers alone.
  3. Confirm the scope and fee. Professional fees are discussed before work begins. Government fees and third-party costs are identified separately where they apply.
  4. Reconcile before filing. GST returns are matched against GSTR-2B and books; income tax returns against Form 26AS and AIS. Mismatches are resolved before submission, not after a notice.
  5. Track the calendar for you. GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, TDS returns, advance tax instalments and ROC annual filings are diarised on your behalf so due dates do not lapse into interest and penalty.

Review before submission

The review depends on the matter. GST returns are matched with the books and GSTR-2B. Income tax returns are checked against Form 26AS and AIS. Company filings are checked against the relevant registers and supporting resolutions. Where the records do not support a proposed position, the issue is raised before filing.

Updates and professional boundaries

Clients receive requests for missing records, filing acknowledgements and notice-related next steps through the agreed communication channel. If a matter requires legal representation, valuation, investment-product distribution or another specialist service outside the engagement, the practice may recommend that the client obtain separate advice.
